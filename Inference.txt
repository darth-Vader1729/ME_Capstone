Analyzing these thermodynamic curves allows you to predict system performance, identify phase boundaries, and optimize energy efficiency in refrigeration cycles. 

SATURATION CURVES

1. Saturation P-T Curves Phase Equilibrium: 

R1234yf. Saturation pressures closely match conventional R134a levels at common evaporator and condenser temperatures. That yields familiar suction and discharge pressures, normal compressor displacement, and easier equipment adaptation. This is why it is widely used as a low GWP R134a replacement.

R1234ze(E). Sits at noticeably lower absolute pressures for the same temperatures. Lower discharge pressure can reduce mechanical stress and sometimes reduce compression temperature. The catch is lower suction pressures and therefore larger volumetric flow. That means different compressor selection.



2. Saturated Vapor & Liquid Enthalpy vs. Temperature Latent Heat Capacity: 

The difference between saturated vapor enthalpy h_g and saturated liquid enthalpy h_f at a given temperature gives the latent heat of vaporization.

R1234ze(E). Shows one of the largest liquid→vapor enthalpy jumps across the temperature range. That means more cooling delivered per kilogram, so lower mass flow for a given duty and smaller piping for the same capacity.

R1234yf. Has a solid, mid-to-high Δh. Not the absolute best per kg, but good enough that mass flows remain moderate while avoiding extreme design changes.


3. T-S Saturation Curves Fluid Classification: 

R1234ze(E). The saturated vapor and liquid lines show a wide horizontal span (large Δs) at typical operating temperatures. That confirms the h–T observation, and implies a large latent component, which tends to improve the refrigeration effect per kg and helps COP for the heat transfer portion of the cycle.

R1234yf. Shows a healthy Δs and a dome shape that keeps compression paths well within the two-phase region and a comfortable distance from the critical point at normal condensing temps. That implies predictable isentropic compression and stable heat exchanger performance.

---------------------------- PRACTICALY FEASIBLE  ---------------------------------

R450A (Solstice® N13) is a non-flammable, low-toxicity (
safety class) zeotropic blend of R134a and R1234ze(E). It serves as a direct, low-GWP (547) drop-in replacement 
for R134a in medium/high-temperature applications, offering
lower capacity but similar COP and reduced energy, making it suitable for chillers and heat pumps. 
Key Details
    Composition: 42% R134a and 58% R1234ze(E).
    Environmental Impact: GWP is 547, which is more than 60% lower than R134a (1430).

Performance: Generally provides similar efficiency (COP) to R134a, with slightly lower capacity in some systems.
Safety: Classified as A1(non-flammable, low toxicity).
Applications: Medium-temperature refrigeration, air-cooled/water-cooled chillers, and heat pumps.
Operational Note: It is a zeotropic mixture with a small temperature glide (~0.4 °C), which should be considered during system design.
